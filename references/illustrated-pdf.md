# Illustrated PDF production

Use this for the default illustrated lecture and when updating an existing illustrated PDF.

1. Map each proposed figure to a reader question and the section where it is answered. Cover the central mechanisms, stage transitions, or empirical comparisons that benefit from a visual explanation. Choose the number of figures from the material. A teaching figure should let the reader follow an input to an output, interpret data, or explain a tradeoff. A decorative cover or a text-only flowchart is insufficient when the mechanism depends on quantities, intermediate states, or timing.
2. Use source figures when the evidence or conventions require them. Use vector circuit diagrams, plots, or schematics for technical content when feasible. Draw from verified source facts, cite the relevant passage, and label invented numbers and simplified examples. Add a caption that tells the reader what to look for. Check labels, units, arrows, encoding, and legibility at the final printed size.
3. Use a paginated layout with embedded fonts for the document language, selectable text, mathematical typesetting, consistent headings, and margins and body type suited to sustained reading. Keep captions with figures and repeat table headers as needed. Pick a PDF tool that supports the source's equations and script, such as a Unicode LaTeX engine or a browser print workflow with locally rendered math.
4. Render every final PDF page to images. Inspect the whole sequence for pagination and white space, then inspect equations, figure labels, tables, and non-Latin glyphs at readable size. Confirm text extraction and links in the exported PDF. Repair problems and rerender the affected output before delivery. Keep the editable source and the figure assets together.
5. If export fails, try another available renderer that preserves text and equations. Report any unresolved output limitation explicitly and link the editable artifact that was produced.
